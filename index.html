Recording paradigm

One conversation, two acoustic viewpoints

Scientific motivation

Is a better acoustic viewpoint itself valuable?

Fixed far-field systems are vulnerable to distance, background noise, reverberation, and overlapping speech. Embodied devices can rotate toward active speakers or approach sound sources, potentially improving direct-to-reverberant ratio and spatial separability.

Movement also introduces self-noise and rapidly varying acoustic transfer functions. EAR-1 isolates this trade-off by using a human proxy to execute a predefined oracle-navigation policy. At evaluation, each track provides a single-channel microphone recording and the corresponding task information.

Navigation is controlled. Speech perception remains the challenge.

Challenge tracks

Two tasks, paired fixed and moving conditions

Each task is evaluated separately on synchronized fixed-microphone (D1) and moving-microphone (R1) recordings of the same conversations. These conditions are also referred to as the Fixed-Array and Moving-Array tracks in earlier challenge materials.

01

ESD-ON

Embodied Speaker Diarization with Oracle Navigation

Assign consistent speaker labels to continuous single-channel recordings, including regions with overlapping speech. Oracle voice activity detection regions are provided without revealing active-speaker identities.

Metric DER Input Audio + oracle VAD
Fixed-Microphone Track · D1 Moving-Microphone Track · R1

ESR-ON

Embodied Speech Recognition with Oracle Navigation

Generate speaker-attributed transcripts from continuous, unsegmented single-channel recordings. Systems jointly handle speech activity detection, speaker attribution, and recognition.

Dataset

Real conversations, paired acoustic viewpoints

The EAR-1 corpus pairs synchronized fixed and moving microphone recordings of real multi-party conversations. The released training and development sets contain 62 sessions with 47 distinct speakers. Training and development speakers do not overlap; evaluation rooms and speakers will be disjoint from both sets.

Training and development materials

  • Synchronized fixed-microphone (D1) and moving-microphone (R1) recordings
  • Auxiliary microphone recordings (H1/H2/H3), participant headset recordings, and fixed-view video
  • Time-aligned transcriptions and speaker activity annotations in TextGrid format
  • For training and development oracle VAD, annotated speech intervals in the per-speaker TextGrid files, such as A/P037.TextGrid

All released WAV files are single-channel recordings. No separate multi-channel audio archive is provided.

Evaluation inputs

  • Fixed-Microphone Track: D1 only. Moving-Microphone Track: R1 only.
  • Oracle VAD regions are also provided for ESD-ON evaluation, without speaker identities.
  • Auxiliary microphone recordings, headset audio, video, trajectories, device poses, and inertial signals are not evaluation inputs.
  • Evaluation speaker labels and reference transcripts remain hidden.

Evaluation

Transparent metrics and separate rankings

Fixed and moving conditions use the same protocols and are ranked independently, enabling controlled within-conversation comparison.

DER

Speaker diarization

Zero forgiveness collar. Overlapping speech is scored. With oracle VAD, the metric mainly reflects speaker confusion and overlap attribution.

cpWER

Speaker-attributed recognition

Speaker-wise hypotheses are concatenated in time and scored under the minimum-error mapping between reference and hypothesized speakers.

Participation

Core rules

01

External resources

Public datasets and pretrained models are allowed when cited, accessible to other teams, and fully disclosed. New resources cannot be introduced after evaluation data release.

02

Permitted inputs

Training may use all released audio, including auxiliary microphones and headset recordings as training material or clean targets. Joint training on D1 and R1 is permitted, and the same model may be submitted to both tracks. At evaluation, the fixed track uses D1 alone and the moving track uses R1 alone, together with the corresponding task information. Trajectories, poses, auxiliary sensors, and organizer-only annotations are prohibited.

03

Final submission

Each entry includes outputs in the official format and a report describing architecture, training, external resources, and decoding configuration.
